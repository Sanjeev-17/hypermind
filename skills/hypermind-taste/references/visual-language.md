# Visual Language

## Source Signal

The initial taste board is image-based and acts as a strong baseline, not the complete definition of Sanjeev's taste. It centers on cultural, material, and archival references: patterned textiles, tilework, ornamental borders, vernacular packaging, Indian poster graphics, regional typography, halftone cinema language, hand-painted illustration, worn surfaces, and layered collage.

Treat taste as multiple dimensions whose weight can change by project and context. Do not turn the baseline into rigid design tokens.

## Taste Principles

- Cultural specificity over generic global polish.
- Material memory over sterile flatness.
- Dense detail with hierarchy over empty minimalism.
- Ornament as a structural language.
- Editorial confidence over timid composition.
- Handmade or printed texture over synthetic smoothness.
- Local signage, packaging, textiles, murals, and everyday artifacts as legitimate design references.

## Recurring Motifs

- Repeating floral, tile, paisley, block-print, and border systems.
- Gridded archives, labels, stamps, seals, badges, and catalog fragments.
- Saturated regional palettes: turmeric, vermilion, indigo, teal, oxide, cream, black, faded paper, and metal.
- Bold mixed typography: condensed Latin display, Devanagari or regional scripts when appropriate, label-like captions, small technical annotations, and oversized poster titles.
- Visible production texture: grain, scuffs, ink spread, imperfect registration, worn edges, creases, ceramic crackle, and oxidized surfaces.

## Translation Notes

Use these references as qualities, not costumes. A fintech dashboard should not become a festival poster; it can borrow confident color, tactile dividers, crisp labeling, and archive-like grouping. A brand surface can take more pattern, illustration, and vernacular typography.

When uncertain, choose one strong expressive system and support it with restraint.

Preserve usability before expressive density: establish function, hierarchy, and interaction clarity before adding surface detail.
