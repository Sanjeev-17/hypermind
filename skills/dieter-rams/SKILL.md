# Dieter Rams — Good Design

Use this skill as a **quality judgment lens**, not a visual style kit. Rams is about whether the design deserves to exist and how little it can be while remaining useful, honest, and clear.

Primary sources: Rams' Ten Principles (Vitsœ / Rams Foundation). Principle wording below follows the public Vitsœ / Rams Foundation formulations (CC-BY-NC-ND for the canonical text — attribute when quoting verbatim).

Motto: **Less, but better** (*Weniger, aber besser*).

## When To Load

- Reviewing whether a UI, site, or product is over-designed
- Checking honesty of claims, metrics, or visual promises
- Deciding what to remove vs what to keep
- Portfolio / case study critique where usefulness and clarity matter more than decoration
- Pairing with `hypermind-taste` when expression threatens usefulness

## Operating Loop

1. Name the product purpose in one sentence.
2. Score or check each of the ten principles (pass / weak / fail).
3. Prefer removals and clarifications over added ornament.
4. Call out dishonesty (inflated metrics, fake novelty, decorative chrome pretending to be function).
5. End with the smallest change set that raises usefulness and clarity.

## Ten Principles

1. **Innovative** — Innovation serves technology and use; never novelty for its own sake.
2. **Useful** — Satisfy functional, psychological, and aesthetic criteria; remove anything that detracts from use.
3. **Aesthetic** — Beauty is part of usefulness because everyday objects affect well-being; only well-executed work can be beautiful.
4. **Understandable** — Clarify structure; ideally self-explanatory; make the product “talk.”
5. **Unobtrusive** — Tools, not art objects; restrained so the user’s content and self-expression lead.
6. **Honest** — Do not overstate power, novelty, or value; no manipulation via unmet promises.
7. **Long-lasting** — Avoid fashion cycles; design that does not age into trend residue.
8. **Thorough** — No arbitrary details; care is respect for the user.
9. **Environmentally friendly** — Conserve resources; minimize physical and visual pollution over the lifecycle.
10. **As little design as possible** — Concentrate on essentials; purity and simplicity.

## Translation To Digital / Portfolio Work

| Principle | Digital application |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Useful | Task completion > spectacle; hiring managers find cases and contact fast |
| Understandable | Hierarchy, labels, IA that explain themselves |
| Unobtrusive | Chrome stays quiet; content and decisions lead |
| Honest | No unverified vanity metrics as hero facts; no fake “AI-powered” theater |
| Thorough | Spacing, states, empty states, focus, mobile — finished, not approximate |
| As little as possible | Cut template leftovers (FAQ/Awards noise) that do not serve the product goal |
| Long-lasting | Prefer durable type/layout systems over seasonal UI fads |
| Environmentally friendly | Fewer assets, less motion waste, durable components, no throwaway pages |

## Review Checklist

- What is the essential job of this screen/object?
- What can be removed without harming that job?
- Where is the design dishonest (claims, visuals, novelty)?
- Is anything fashionable that will look dated in 18 months?
- Are details intentional or leftover template noise?
- Does aesthetic quality support calm use, or compete with it?
- Would Rams keep this control/decoration?

## Anti-Patterns

- Treating Rams as “make everything gray and flat Apple-like”
- Using “less but better” to excuse incomplete research or weak hierarchy
- Stripping cultural or expressive identity when the brief needs it — apply restraint to **chrome**, not to meaning
- Performing minimalism while shipping unused sections and empty modules

## Pairing

- With **Don Norman**: Rams judges *worthiness and restraint*; Norman judges *discoverability and interaction psychology*.
- With **hypermind-taste**: taste supplies cultural/expressive dialect; Rams decides how much of it the product may carry.
